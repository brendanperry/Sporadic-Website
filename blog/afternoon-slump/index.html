Why you're tired in the afternoon, and how to beat the 3pm slump

The afternoon slump is mostly your body clock. Your alertness naturally dips in the early afternoon, and it happens even if you skip lunch, though a big lunch and a bad night's sleep make it worse [1]. Getting up and moving is one of the best quick fixes. In one study, 10 minutes of easy stair walking boosted people's energy more than a 50 mg dose of caffeine [2]. In another, 3 minutes of light walking every 30 minutes left people less tired later in the day than sitting straight through [3].

Why am I so tired in the afternoon?

Your body has a natural dip in alertness in the early afternoon. Sleep researchers describe the so-called post-lunch dip as largely unrelated to lunch [1]. You'd feel it at 2 or 3pm even if you skipped the meal.

That doesn't mean lunch is off the hook. In the same study, people did a long, boring drive in a simulator after either a light lunch or a heavy one. The heavy lunch made them drift out of their lane more and showed more signs of sleepiness in their brainwave readings [1]. The researchers also found a bad night's sleep makes the dip worse.

So the slump is a mix of 3 things. Your body clock sets it up, and a big lunch and short sleep make it hit harder. Sitting still in front of a screen for hours doesn't help either.

Does moving help more than coffee?

For a quick boost, it might. A 2017 study had 18 young women who were short on sleep try 3 different things on different days [2]. One day they took a 50 mg caffeine pill, about what's in a can of some sodas. Another day they took a placebo. On the third, they walked up and down stairs at an easy pace for 10 minutes.

The stair walking gave them a bigger jump in how energetic they felt than the caffeine did. The boost didn't last forever though. An hour later, the stair and caffeine groups felt about the same. And neither one made a difference on memory or attention tests.

It's a small study, and 50 mg is a lot less caffeine than a cup of coffee, so I wouldn't throw out your coffee maker. But it's a good reason to try a walk first.

Does breaking up sitting help with afternoon fatigue?

Another study had 19 adults sit for a full 7-hour day in a lab, twice [3]. One day they sat straight through. The other day they got up every 30 minutes for 3 minutes of light walking.

On the walking day, people reported feeling less tired at the 4-hour and 7-hour marks. That's right when most of us are fighting the afternoon slump. Like the stair study, it didn't find any change on memory or thinking tests. So moving made people feel less wiped out, but it didn't turn them into geniuses.

That's still a win. Feeling less tired at 3pm is the whole point.

Why "I'll just get up at 3" doesn't happen

The trouble with the afternoon slump is that it's the exact moment you least feel like moving. You're tired, you've got 4 tabs open, and getting up sounds like a lot of work. So you grab another coffee or open your phone instead.

Frequently asked questions

What time is the afternoon slump?

For most people it hits somewhere between 1 and 4pm, often around 2 or 3. It depends a lot on when you woke up and how well you slept. If you're an early riser, it'll probably show up earlier. The dip is part of your body clock, so it happens even on days you skip lunch.

Is the afternoon slump caused by lunch?

Not mostly. Sleep researchers describe the post-lunch dip as largely unrelated to lunch itself. That said, a heavy lunch does make it worse. In one driving study, people who ate a big lunch were sleepier and drifted out of their lane more than people who ate a light one.

Is it better to walk or drink coffee for an afternoon slump?

Try the walk first. In one small study, 10 minutes of easy stair walking gave a bigger energy boost than 50 mg of caffeine, though the effect faded within an hour. You can always do both. A walk to the coffee shop counts!

How do I stay awake at my desk in the afternoon?

Get up every 30-60 minutes, even for a minute or two. In one lab study, 3 minutes of light walking every half hour left people less tired later in the day. Stand for calls, take the stairs, or do 10 squats next to your desk. Saving easier tasks for the afternoon helps too.

Should I take a nap to beat the afternoon slump?

If you can, a short one can help. Keep it short and set an alarm so you don't wake up groggy. Most of us can't nap at work though, which is why moving is the more practical fix. And if you need a nap every single afternoon, it's worth looking at your sleep.
