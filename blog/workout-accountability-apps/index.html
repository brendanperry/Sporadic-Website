The best workout accountability apps, and which kind fits you

The best workout accountability app depends on what keeps you going. Sweatmates pairs you with one partner and a small wager if you miss your weekly goal. GymRats runs group challenges with photo posts and a leaderboard. WayBetter and Forfeit put a consequence on the line, money or a message to a friend. Sporadic, which we make, combines reminders with friends: it tells you when to move with short random challenges and a deadline, and your friends can join the same stack so you keep a streak going together. All five are free to download with in-app purchases.

The 4 kinds of workout accountability

Every accountability app is doing one of 4 things. Figure out which one works on you, and the choice gets a lot easier.

The first is a partner. One person sees whether you showed up. The second is a group. A bunch of friends or coworkers, usually with a leaderboard. The third is a consequence. Money, a wager, or an embarrassing message if you miss. The fourth is a nudge. Something tells you when to go, so you don't have to remember.

The first 3 check up on you after the fact. The last one works before you've had the chance to skip. Most people need a little of both, and some apps mix them. Sporadic, for example, is a nudge and a group: it reminds you when to move, and your friends can join the same stack and keep a streak with you.

Sweatmates: best if you have one gym buddy

Sweatmates is built around a single partner [1]. You both set a weekly goal for how many days you'll work out, and you log each workout with a photo your partner can see right away. If you miss your goal, you owe them something small, like dinner or a chore.

It's simple, and the wager is a fun twist. The listing says it skips stats and metrics on purpose, so it's about showing up, not tracking your lifts. Workout logging needs a subscription.

GymRats: best for a group challenge

GymRats is the one to grab if you've got a group chat full of people who want to compete [2]. You create a challenge, invite as many people as you want, and everyone posts their workouts with a photo. There's a leaderboard, and the group picks how to score it, whether that's number of workouts, minutes, miles, calories, steps, or your own point system.

Any activity counts, and it can pull in workouts from Apple Health and Apple Watch. It's a great fit for an office fitness challenge.

WayBetter and Forfeit: best if you need stakes

Some people only follow through when something's on the line. That's who these two are for.

WayBetter runs games around fitness, nutrition, and stress, and you can choose to put money on the line or bring in an accountability partner [3]. Forfeit isn't fitness-specific. You set any habit with a deadline and a consequence, and then prove you did it with a photo that's checked by AI, a timelapse, a friend's sign-off, or data from Apple Health [4].

If you're the type who'll find a loophole in anything, the verification options in Forfeit are hard to beat.

Sporadic: best if your problem is getting started

Full disclosure, Sporadic is our app, so take this section with that in mind.

Sporadic works differently from everything above. The other apps check whether you worked out. Sporadic tells you when to move. You pick a stack, like Daily Push-Ups or Office Breaks, and we send you short challenges at random times through the day, like 20 push-ups or a set of stretches. Each one comes with a deadline. Finish them to keep your streak alive, and invite friends so they see it too [5].

It's not a workout tracker, and it won't log your gym sessions. Think of it as a supplement. For a lot of us it's what gets us off our butts on the days a real workout doesn't happen.

Frequently asked questions

Do workout accountability apps work?

They can, if you pick the kind that matches what makes you skip. If you forget, you need a reminder. If you lose interest, a group or a partner helps. If you talk yourself out of it, stakes work. An app that checks up on you after the fact won't do much if the real problem is never getting started.

What is the best free workout accountability app?

All 5 apps here are free to download, but what's free varies. GymRats is free with an optional Pro upgrade. Sporadic is free to start, with in-app purchases. Sweatmates needs a subscription to log workouts. Check each listing for current pricing, since it changes.

Is there an app that makes you pay if you skip a workout?

Yes. WayBetter lets you put money on the line in its games, and Forfeit lets you set a consequence that triggers if you miss a deadline. Sweatmates uses a friendlier version, where you owe your partner something small like dinner if you miss your weekly goal.

What app can I use for an office fitness challenge?

GymRats is built for group challenges with a leaderboard, and it can score by workouts, minutes, steps, or your own points. If you want something everyone can do at their desk, Sporadic lets you invite coworkers to the same stack so you all get the same random challenges through the day.

Can I use more than one accountability app?

Sure. They do different jobs. A partner or group app works well for your main workouts, and a nudge app like Sporadic covers the hours in between. Just don't sign up for so many that checking them becomes its own chore.
